Free and open source · macOS 14 or later · Apple silicon (M1 and newer)

Three steps, no fuss

It lives in your menu bar and stays out of the way until you need it.

1

Press ⌃⌥⌘K

Or click the keycap in your menu bar, or run keypause start in Terminal.

2

Wipe away

Every key is off, including shortcuts and media keys. Your mouse and trackpad still work, so Unlock is one click away.

3

Keys come back

When the timer ends the keyboard switches itself back on and the little island tucks away.

Made to be pleasant

A utility you open once a month still deserves some care.

A night that ends in sunrise

No progress bar. Kip the keycap naps while the sky turns from night to dawn, then wakes up just before your keys return.

Never in your way

The HUD doesn't steal focus, so a video keeps playing while you clean. The screen stays awake too.

Grows out of the notch

On a notched MacBook the island grows out of the notch itself. Everywhere else it's a neat pill under the menu bar.

Always ends

Pick 30 seconds to 10 minutes. The timer always runs out, and Unlock is always on screen.

Scriptable

A real keypause command, plus keypause:// links for Raycast and Shortcuts.

Private by design

Keypause never reads, stores or sends your keystrokes. Its only network request is an optional daily update check.

From Terminal

Homebrew puts the keypause command on your PATH.

$ keypause start # your chosen duration
$ keypause start 90 # 90 seconds ("2m" works too)
$ keypause stop # unlock early
$ keypause check # check for updates

Homebrew

On first run Keypause asks for permission to control your keyboard. The welcome window walks you through it.

Questions

Why does it need Accessibility permission?

macOS only lets an app intercept key presses if you allow it under Privacy & Security. Keypause uses that to drop key events before apps see them. It never looks at what you type.

macOS says it can't verify the app

Keypause isn't notarized because that needs a paid Apple developer account. Installing with Homebrew skips the warning. For a direct download, right-click the app and choose Open the first time, or run:

xattr -dr com.apple.quarantine /Applications/Keypause.app
It's switched on but macOS still asks for permission

This can happen after an update. The welcome window has a "Reset and retry" button, or you can reset it from Terminal and grant it again:

tccutil reset Accessibility com.destucr.Keypause
tccutil reset ListenEvent com.destucr.Keypause
Can it block the power button?

No. The power and Touch ID button are handled by the hardware, so no software can switch them off.

Does it phone home?

Only for an optional update check against this project's public GitHub releases, at most once a day, and it sends nothing about you. Turn it off from the menu with Check Automatically.
